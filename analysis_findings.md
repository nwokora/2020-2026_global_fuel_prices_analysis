## Dataset Coverage
- The analysis covered 84 countries across 7 continental regions.

## Fuel Price Patterns
### Regional fuel price distribution
- Europe has the highest average fuel price at 3.33.
- Oceania follows closely at 3.29.
- The Middle East has the lowest average fuel price at 1.10.
- South America and Africa also record relatively low averages at 1.26 and 1.38.

### Country-level fuel price extremes
- Europe contains 8 of the top 10 countries with the highest average fuel prices.
- The Middle East and Africa dominate the top 10 countries with the lowest average fuel prices.

### Yearly fuel price trend
- Average fuel prices increased from 2020 to 2026.
- There was a small dip in 2023, after which prices rose again.

## Tax Burden Patterns
### Regional tax burden
- Oceania has the highest average tax percentage at about 39%.
- Europe follows at about 35%.
- South America and the Middle East have lower averages at 18% and 20%.

### Highest taxpayers
- Rwanda has the highest tax percentage at 39.6%.
- Europe contains 7 of the top 10 highest-taxing countries.
- Africa has 4 countries among the lowest taxpayers, followed by the Middle East with 2 countries.

## Subsidy Distribution
### Regional subsidy distribution
- The Middle East has the highest average subsidy level at 3.00.
- South America follows at 2.43.
- Africa averages 2.00.
- Europe and Oceania have the lowest averages at 1.17 and 1.00.

### Subsidy level distribution by region
- Oceania has 100% low subsidy.
- Europe has 86.96% low subsidy.
- The Middle East shows the strongest support, with 55.56% very high subsidies.
- Low-income affordability under subsidy levels
- For low-income countries, affordability improves as subsidy level increases.
- Countries such as Venezuela, Libya, Egypt, and Nigeria are the most favoured under very high subsidies.
- Uganda, Mozambique, Zambia, Rwanda, and Zimbabwe are the least favoured and more likely to face stronger affordability pressure.

## Income Level Distribution
- Oceania is the most favoured, with 100% high-income countries.
- Europe follows with about 74% high-income countries.
- North America has 66.67% high-income countries.
- The Middle East has 55.56% high-income countries.
- Africa is the least favoured by income structure, with 73.91% low-income countries, 26.09% middle-income countries, and 0% high-income countries.

Findings
The analysis covered 84 countries across 7 continental regions.

Europe has the highest average fuel price at 3.33, followed by Oceania at 3.29. The Middle East has the lowest average fuel price at 1.10, with South America and Africa also recording relatively low averages of 1.26 and 1.38.

Europe dominates the top 10 countries with the highest average fuel prices, while the Middle East and Africa dominate the top 10 countries with the lowest average fuel prices.

Average fuel prices increased from 2020 to 2026, with a slight dip in 2023 before rising again.

Oceania has the highest average tax percentage at about 39%, followed by Europe at about 35%. South America and the Middle East have the lowest averages at 18% and 20%, respectively.

Rwanda has the highest tax percentage at 39.6%.

Europe contains 7 of the top 10 highest-taxing countries, while Africa has 4 countries among the lowest taxpayers, followed by the Middle East with 2 countries.

The Middle East has the highest average subsidy level at 3.00, followed by South America at 2.43 and Africa at 2.00. Europe and Oceania have the lowest averages at 1.17 and 1.00, respectively.

Oceania has 100% low subsidy, while the Middle East has the strongest subsidy support, with 55.56% very high subsidies.

For low-income countries with very high subsidies, Venezuela, Libya, Egypt, and Nigeria are the most favoured in fuel affordability, while Uganda, Mozambique, Zambia, Rwanda, and Zimbabwe are the least favoured.

On income level distribution, Oceania is the most favoured, with 100% high-income countries. Europe follows with about 74% high-income countries, then North America with 66.67%, and the Middle East with 55.56%.

Africa is the least favoured by income structure, with 73.91% low-income countries, 26.09% middle-income countries, and 0% high-income countries.
