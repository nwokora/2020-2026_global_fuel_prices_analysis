# 💡 Analysis Insights

## Executive Summary

This analysis examined 84 countries across seven continental regions to assess global fuel price patterns, tax burden, and subsidy distribution. The results show clear regional inequalities in fuel affordability, with Europe and Oceania generally facing the highest fuel prices, tax burdens, and low subsidy support, while the Middle East records the lowest fuel prices and the strongest subsidy support.

## ⛽ Regional Fuel Price Averages
Fuel prices vary sharply across regions, with Europe recording the highest average fuel price at 3.33 and Oceania following closely at 3.29. The Middle East has the lowest average at 1.10, while South America and Africa remain relatively low at 1.26 and 1.38, respectively. At the country level, Europe accounts for 8 of the top 10 highest fuel-price countries, while the Middle East and Africa account for the lowest fuel-price countries.

## 💼 Income Level by Region
Income structure helps explain why some regions absorb fuel costs more easily than others. Oceania has 100% high-income countries, while Europe has about 74%, North America has 67%, and the Middle East has 56%. Africa is the least favoured by income structure, with 74% low-income countries, 26% middle-income countries, and 0% high-income countries. This suggests that higher-income concentration may cushion the burden of high fuel prices and taxes in regions like Europe and Oceania.

## 💰 Regional Subsidy Averages
Regional average subsidy levels show the overall strength of subsidy support, with the Middle East recording the highest average at 3.00 and Oceania the lowest at 1.00. However, the regional subsidy distribution gives a more detailed picture of how those averages are formed: Oceania is made up entirely of low-subsidy countries, Europe is also dominated by low subsidies, while the Middle East has the largest share of very high subsidies. This shows that the Middle East does not just have a high average subsidy score; it also has many countries concentrated in the highest subsidy category.

## 💰 Low-Income Countries by Subsidy Level
Among low-income countries, the best performers — Venezuela, Libya, Egypt, and Nigeria — are supported by very high subsidies, which help improve fuel affordability. The worst performers — Uganda, Mozambique, Zambia, Rwanda, and Zimbabwe — are supported by low subsidies, which makes affordability more difficult.


## 🧾 Regional Tax Percentage Averages
Tax burden is highest in Oceania at about 39%, followed by Europe at about 35%. South America and the Middle East have lower average tax percentages at 18% and 20%, respectively. Europe contains 7 of the top 10 highest-taxing countries. This shows that tax pressure is strongest in regions already facing high fuel costs.

Taken together, the findings show that the interaction of fuel prices, income levels, subsidies, and taxes shapes global fuel affordability. Europe and Oceania face the greatest cost pressure, while the Middle East benefits from lower prices and stronger subsidies. Africa faces the most difficulty overall because of lower income levels and weaker support in many cases.
